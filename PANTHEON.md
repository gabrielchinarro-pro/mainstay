## Why this text ships today

The Pantheon has been running internally for a while now. The announcement was set for September, once the engine had been broken in on enough deliveries for every rule to cite the incident that produced it — that was the condition I had set myself before publishing anything.

On 31 August 2026, Nous Research shipped Hermes Agent v0.21.0 under the title **"The Pantheon Release"**: agent identities, agent-to-agent communication, subagent orchestration steerable mid-flight.

I am not comparing myself to Nous Research. They have the models, the means and the reach; I am an operator who ships projects with agents, every day, for real clients.

**The AI giants' calendar is forcing me to accelerate publication.** So I am shipping the facade now, incomplete and owned as such, rather than in September looking like the one who followed.

## What I claim, and what I do not

**I do not claim the idea of making agents collaborate.** It is public, worked on by many, and far better funded elsewhere than here. Getting agents to talk to each other stopped being an invention a long time ago.

**I claim the law that governs them.** A protocol where every function carries a single name, where every act of execution has a written form, where irreversible gates are never crossed without explicit human consent, and where every rule cites the incident that produced it. Not a framework: a body of case law.

The difference fits in one sentence. **Getting agents to talk to each other is an engineering problem. Stopping them from doing damage in your name is a legal one.** The second is mine.

## The acts — the method

| Act | What it is | The rule |
|---|---|---|
| **The Augury** | The announcement before execution | Nothing launches without reading the auspices: problem detected, team summoned, gates planned, what is expected of the human. Five lines, before the first action. |
| **The Arena** | The adversarial loop | You do not exit on "looks good". You exit on a dry pass confirmed by a pass from fresh angles — or on an honest freeze, written down. |
| **The Rubicon** | The irreversible gates | Push, deploy, send, account creation, server writes. Nobody crosses them without explicit human consent. **No override beats the Rubicon** — not even the one who wrote it. |
| **The Tables** | The engraved decisions | Numbered, dated, attributed. You supersede; you never erase. A reversed decision stays readable next to what reversed it. |
| **The Torch** | The handover | State passes from one session to the next without falling: measured, not inferred; decisions locked; first action ordered. What is stale is marked stale. |
| **The Standard** | The session status | Says **who holds the ball** — the human, the outside world, the agent fleet, or nobody — never the progress. An operator running ten sessions sees at a glance the three that are waiting on them. |

## The roles — one name per function

The founding rule fits in one line: **one name per function, never two.** What it really forbids is the opposite — the "agent thingy", redefined every session, meaning nothing by the third.

A compact vocabulary is infrastructure. *"Nemesis on the diff"* is a complete specification in four words: who steps in, on what, under which mandate.

| Name | Function |
|---|---|
| **Janus** | The two-faced orchestrator: one face reads the problem, the other reads the project. Opens, routes, closes. |
| **Hephaestus** | The forge. Produces the code, and **never judges its own work**. |
| **Athena** | Strategy and arbitration. Cuts the increments, keeps the register of gaps, prepares the gates. Writes no code. |
| **Momus** | The god of blame. Contests everything, fixes nothing. A reasoned opinion is mandatory — including "nothing to report". |
| **Argus** | A hundred eyes that never sleep. Mechanical, repeatable verification. |
| **Proteus** | Every shape. Exploration and cross-cutting work: whatever fits no charter. |
| **Nemesis** | The retribution of hubris. Refutes claims of perfection, and shadows the forge on any code not yet accepted. |
| **The Watchman** | The night round. A **script**, mechanical, not an agent — decided that way after a reasoned adversarial refusal: unsupervised monitoring must not be able to interpret anything. |

## The law of separation

One rule holds the whole edifice up: **a stage is never closed by whoever built it.**

Every piece of work passes at least one independent judge, and judges fix nothing — which is what stops them from becoming producers in turn. The forge does not sign off on the forge. The contester does not repair what it contests. Acceptance does not negotiate its own criteria.

This is what separates a team of agents from one talkative agent. **A model reviewing its own work finds what it already thought.** A model mandated to refute it finds something else. Separation is not org-chart politeness: it is the only thing that produces real contradiction instead of expensive agreement.

## What is not published, and why

This document is the minimal version — the foundations, and nothing more. What stays private:

- **the routing** — the cascade that reads a problem and derives on its own the type, the project profile, the team to summon and the gates to set;
- **the modules and compositions** — one playbook per problem type, from a ticket to an incident;
- **the role charters** — each name's full mandate, what it may and may not do;
- **the case law** — the corpus of decisions, violations and proofs.

This is not commercial hoarding, it is an observation. **These rules are case law: each one cites the incident that produced it.** Cloning the files would not clone the incidents, and without them the rules lose their teeth — they revert to best practices you read and never apply. What has value here is not the wording of the rules, it is what made them non-negotiable.

So this document tells you **what the upper floors do, not how to rebuild them.**
